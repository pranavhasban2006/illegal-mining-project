1 Introduction and Motivation

Illegal and unregulated mining is among the most significant drivers of anthropogenic land degradation in resource-rich developing and emerging economies. Its consequences extend beyond direct excavation footprints to encompass large-scale deforestation, irreversible topsoil and hydrological disturbance, heavy-metal and sediment-based contamination of downstream water bodies, and the destabilisation of protected and indigenous territories. In many jurisdictions, illegal operations are deliberately established in remote, low-accessibility terrain specifically to evade conventional ground-based regulatory inspection, rendering manual, field-survey-driven monitoring both logistically infeasible and economically unsustainable at a national or continental scale.

The proliferation of freely accessible, high-revisit-frequency Earth Observation (EO) constellations — most notably the Copernicus Sentinel-2 multispectral mission, accessible through the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem — has fundamentally altered the feasibility of continuous, wide-area environmental surveillance. However, the raw volume, spectral complexity, and temporal density of this data render manual visual interpretation impractical beyond localised case studies. This motivates the central problem statement addressed by the present project: the design and implementation of an automated, satellite-driven AI pipeline capable of (i) detecting candidate mining-disturbed land, (ii) quantifying spatio-temporal change indicative of active excavation, (iii) isolating unpermitted or suspicious extraction footprints from legally licensed operations, and (iv) generating actionable, geo-referenced alerts for regulatory authorities.

This document constitutes Project Review Document 1 and establishes the academic and technical foundation for the proposed system through a structured review of five research contributions published in 2026, each directly relevant to satellite-based change detection, surface mining surveillance, or hybrid geospatial deep learning architectures. The review is intended to substantiate the architectural and methodological choices adopted in the implemented prototype, and to be evaluated by the university review panel against the standard of current research in the domain.

2 Problem Statement

Conventional monitoring of surface mining activity relies predominantly on periodic manual inspection, community reporting, and ad hoc visual interpretation of satellite imagery by regulatory personnel. This approach does not scale to the spatial extent and temporal frequency required to detect illegal excavation before substantial ecological damage has occurred, particularly in low-accessibility forested or riverine terrain where illegal operations are disproportionately concentrated. The literature reviewed in Section 3 confirms that deep-learning-based segmentation and change-detection models can reliably automate this task at a per-pixel level; however, no single reviewed architecture jointly addresses all four functional requirements of a deployable monitoring pipeline — candidate-area detection, temporal change quantification, licensed-versus-unlicensed footprint discrimination, and actionable alert generation. This project formulates its problem statement as the design and prototype implementation of an integrated pipeline that combines these four stages into a single, Sentinel-2-driven workflow.

3 Literature Review

Five 2026 research contributions were reviewed, spanning three themes directly relevant to this project's architecture: mining-specific change detection, a dedicated benchmark for illegal mining in the exact biome and activity type this project targets, and methodological choices (lightweight architectures, class-imbalance handling) that this project's implementation adopts directly. Venue type is marked explicitly below — one contribution is a peer-reviewed journal article, three are 2026 arXiv preprints (standard practice for citing current work in fast-moving ML subfields), and one is an operational industry deployment report rather than an academic publication.

3.1 Bi-temporal change detection for open-pit mining

Global–Local Feature Fusion Network for Remote Sensing Image Change Detection in Open-Pit Mining Areas Peer-reviewed
Zheng, Z., Yang, J., Lv, G., Li, Q., Wang, Y. · Sensors 26(10), 2026 · DOI: 10.3390/s26103128

Proposes GLMECD-Net, a Siamese-encoder change-detection network purpose-built for open-pit mining regions, where mining-related changes exhibit "multi-scale patterns, irregular boundaries, and fragmented spatial distributions" that generic change-detection architectures handle poorly. A global–local fusion module jointly captures long-range context and local spatial detail across two temporal images, achieving 83.78% overall accuracy and 77.53% F1 on benchmark open-pit datasets.

Relevance to this project

Directly validates O3's premise — that mining-area change detection needs treatment distinct from generic land-cover change detection. This project's change_detection.py addresses the same irregular/fragmented-footprint problem via per-scene probability normalization and connected-component blob filtering rather than a learned fusion network, trading architectural sophistication for a dramatically smaller labeled-data requirement (see Section 5).

3.2 A dedicated benchmark for illegal Amazon gold mining

ELDOR: A Dataset and Benchmark for Illegal Gold Mining in the Amazon Rainforest arXiv preprint
Cui, K., Bohara, S., Prasai, S., Shao, Z., Tang, W., Pillaca, M., Flores, E., Yang, Z., Larsen, G., Dethier, E., Lutz, D., Morel, J.-M., Silman, M., Pauca, V., Yang, F. · arXiv:2605.15397, May 2026

Introduces ELDOR, a UAV-based orthomosaic dataset covering over 2,500 hectares with pixel-level semantic labels for mining activity and surrounding ecological structure, spanning four evaluation tasks (segmentation, recognition, multi-label classification, vision-language). The authors report that "current methods still struggle with rare small-scale mining structures and fine-grained recovery classes" — exactly the artisanal, fragmented mining pattern documented in this project's pilot region.

Relevance to this project

Corroborates this project's own empirical finding (Section 6.3): a model trained on the "easy" extremes of the mining/non-mining spectrum systematically under-performs on rare, small, or ambiguous cases — the exact "small-scale mining structures" failure mode ELDOR documents. This project's response (hard-case labeling and retraining, hard_case_labels.py) is a lightweight, tabular-classifier analogue of the same problem ELDOR's benchmark is designed to expose in segmentation models.

3.3 Architecture-efficiency trade-offs in satellite segmentation

When Less Is More: A Controlled Benchmark of Lightweight CNNs for Satellite Land-Cover Segmentation on DeepGlobe arXiv preprint
Rehman, A. U., Donovan, J. M. · arXiv:2607.23024, July 2026

A controlled comparison of five CNN architectures under identical preprocessing/training protocols finds that MobileNetV2 (24.98 MB) achieves 79.06% accuracy and 0.4625 mean IoU on DeepGlobe land-cover segmentation, outperforming substantially larger models including VGG16 (71.13 MB) and InceptionV3 (125.17 MB). Critically, the authors find that "architectural optimization alone cannot optimize spectrally similar minority classes," with persistent rangeland–barren confusion regardless of model size.

Relevance to this project

Directly supports this project's core architectural decision (Section 5.1): given no labeled segmentation dataset exists for this specific AOI, a lightweight, fast-training classifier is preferable to a heavy segmentation CNN, and — per this paper's own finding — a bigger model would not by itself solve the spectral-confusion problem this project encountered empirically (moderate vegetation loss vs. mining, Section 6.3). This is external confirmation that the project's fix (real hard-negative labels, not a larger model) targets the correct failure mode.

3.4 An operational precedent: continent-scale deployment

Amazon Mining Watch: AI-Powered, Continent-Wide Gold Mining Deforestation Monitoring Industry deployment
Amazon Conservation Association, in partnership with regional monitoring organizations · reported via Mongabay, April 2026

A deployed (not merely research-stage) AI system applying "a unified methodology for the whole Amazon" to detect mining-driven deforestation via satellite imagery, publishing quarterly reports across all nine Amazonian nations. Reports 6,000 ha of Q4-2025 mining deforestation and 496,000 ha cumulatively since 2018, with Peru specifically accounting for 1,700 ha in the most recent quarter. The platform also estimates the monetary socio-environmental cost of detected mining.

Relevance to this project

Establishes both the feasibility and the real-world demand for exactly this project's problem statement at operational scale, and provides an independent, real-world magnitude check: this project's pilot-AOI detection (Section 6.4, ~1,519 ha/year illegal disturbance in a single tile over La Pampa) is consistent in order of magnitude with Amazon Mining Watch's reported Peru-wide quarterly figures, lending external plausibility to the pipeline's output.

3.5 Class-imbalance handling for minority-class detection

QC-SMOTE: Quality-Controlled SMOTE for Imbalanced Classification arXiv preprint
Upman, P., Gowda, S. N. · arXiv:2606.24625, June 2026

Proposes a quality-controlled extension to standard SMOTE that scores minority-sample trustworthiness via a composite neighbourhood metric (local density, safe-level, isolation from the majority class) before generating synthetic examples, with low-quality synthetic samples replaced by minority duplicates in noisy regions. Across 30 imbalanced benchmark datasets, QC-SMOTE improves average AUC-ROC and Macro F1 over standard oversampling, with the largest gains under moderate-to-severe imbalance — precisely the regime mining-pixel detection sits in (mining pixels are a small minority of any scene).

Relevance to this project

Directly informs O2's methodology: this project's model_selection.py already applies standard SMOTE to the training split before comparing algorithms, on exactly the class-imbalance regime (mining vs. non-mining pixels) this paper targets. QC-SMOTE is identified as the concrete next upgrade path — standard SMOTE can generate synthetic minority samples in low-density or noisy regions of feature space (e.g. near the ambiguous-vegetation confusion documented in Section 6.3), which is exactly the failure mode QC-SMOTE's trustworthiness scoring is designed to suppress.

4 Project Objectives

  • O1Data Pipeline: Acquire and preprocess multi-temporal Sentinel-2 Level-2A imagery via the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem for one or more pilot regions exhibiting known or suspected illegal mining activity.
  • O2Mining-Area Detection: Implement a semantic segmentation model capable of delineating mining-disturbed terrain from surrounding land cover with a target Intersection-over-Union (IoU) benchmarked against the 2026 literature baselines summarised in Table 1.
  • O3Change Detection: Implement a bi-temporal change-detection mechanism to isolate newly disturbed or expanding excavation footprints between successive observation dates.
  • O4Suspicious-Area Isolation: Cross-reference detected footprints against publicly available licensed-mining-lease boundaries (where obtainable) to generate a confidence-scored suspicious-area heatmap distinguishing probable illegal activity from permitted operations.
  • O5Alert Generation: Export detections as geo-referenced GeoJSON polygons with associated confidence metrics, suitable for ingestion by a downstream alert-notification mechanism for regulatory stakeholders.

5 Implemented Architecture

Per Section 2's gap analysis, no single reviewed architecture jointly addresses all four functional stages. The implemented prototype integrates them into one Sentinel-2-driven pipeline, deliberately favoring a tabular spectral-feature classifier over a segmentation CNN for O2 (justified directly by [3]'s finding that architectural scale does not resolve spectral-confusion failure modes, and by the complete absence of a labeled segmentation dataset for this specific AOI).

5.2 Ground truth: real government data, not synthetic labels

O2's classifier is trained on real ground truth sourced from RAISG (Red Amazónica de Información Socioambiental Georreferenciada) mining-concession polygons, not synthetic or self-referential percentile-threshold labels. Legal and illegal mining share an identical excavated-bare-soil spectral signature — only administrative status differs — so active/titled concession interiors are a genuine positive-label source, and land more than 300 m from any concession with high NDVI is a genuine negative source. O4 reuses these same real concession polygons, extended with a three-tier legal-status distinction (active / frozen–pending / none) that matches Peruvian mining-concession law more precisely than a binary licensed/unlicensed split.

6.3 Independent validation on ambiguous cases

Beyond the algorithm-comparison metrics above — which measure performance on the "easy" extremes of the training distribution — a stratified sample of 40 genuinely ambiguous locations (near-concession boundaries, river sandbars, the model's own 50/50-probability cases, undiscovered bare patches, mid-range vegetation) was hand labeled via a purpose-built shared labeling tool. This surfaced a real, specific weakness: the model over-flagged moderate vegetation loss (fallow land, agriculture, regrowth) as mining, traced to feature importances that ranked NDVI/NDWI above the more mining-specific BSI/NDMI signal — consistent with [3]'s finding that this class of spectral confusion is architecture-independent.

Fix and honest evaluation Rather than hand-tuning feature weights, the hand labels were folded directly into training (stratified 50/50 split, no leakage between the training and evaluation halves). F1 on the held-out half improved from 0.833 to 0.909 (precision 0.714 → 0.833, recall held at a perfect 1.000).

6.4 External plausibility check

The pilot AOI's detected illegal-mining rate (~1,519 ha/year in a single Sentinel-2 tile) is consistent in order of magnitude with Amazon Mining Watch's independently reported Peru-wide quarterly figures [4] (~1,700 ha/quarter nationally), providing an external sanity check against a deployed real-world system rather than only internal cross-validation.

7 Limitations and Threats to Validity

  1. Sample size on hard-case validation. The 40-chip ambiguous-case evaluation (Section 6.3) currently reflects a single labeler; inter-rater agreement has not yet been measured. The labeling tool supports additional labelers on the same chip set without code changes.
  2. Seasonal phenology confound (resolved). An initial June-vs-August comparison conflated dry-season vegetation stress with mining-driven land-cover change (verified: scene-wide NDVI dropped 0.48→0.32 with no plausible mining cause). Resolved by comparing the same calendar date one year apart instead, which holds sun angle and seasonal phenology approximately constant.
  3. Single pilot AOI. All quantitative results are from one Sentinel-2 tile over Madre de Dios; generalization to other mining biomes (e.g. hard-rock mining in arid terrain, which produces a different spectral signature than Amazonian alluvial gold mining) has not been tested.
  4. Image-based CNN/GAN pipeline not implemented. A parallel deep learning track (CLAHE contrast normalization → GAN-based augmentation → CNN/U-Net segmentation) was scoped but deferred: it requires a labeled image-patch dataset that does not yet exist for this AOI, and [3]'s finding that larger models do not resolve the specific spectral-confusion failure mode observed here reduces its expected marginal value relative to the effort required.

8 Future Work

  • Recruit additional labelers on the existing 40-chip hard-case set to establish inter-rater reliability before treating validation metrics as conclusive.
  • Adopt QC-SMOTE [5] in place of standard SMOTE, targeting the noisy-boundary region around the ambiguous-vegetation confusion identified in Section 6.3.
  • Extend the pilot to a second AOI with a structurally different mining signature (e.g. hard-rock/quarry mining) to test generalization.
  • Acquire finer-grained illegal-mining ground truth (RAISG's own illegal-mining layer was too coarse for this AOI — a single hand-drawn 2016 regional outline, not per-pixel footprints) to validate O4's illegal-classification independently of the concession-based proxy.
  • Phase 2, contingent on labeled image data becoming available: CLAHE-normalized, GAN-augmented CNN segmentation as a direct upgrade path for O2, using the same feature-stack/probability-map interface so O3–O5 require no changes.
